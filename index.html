Ten Acres

CAL FIRE’s stated goal is to keep 95 of every 100 wildfires at 10 acres or less. This page reads the public record to show what happens to the other five, what a dispatcher can actually know in a fire’s first hour, and how to tell when an AI copilot is claiming more than the record supports.

    Check the copilot

    The thresholds are a design choice, not a validated rule. Inside the 95% interval of the deepest precedent pool reads as consistent. Up to three times beyond it asks for a stated reason. Further than that is treated as an extraordinary claim. A copilot that sees live wind and fuel moisture can be right to depart from the record. It should just have to say why.

    06

    What the record cannot see

    That ceiling is the useful finding. Place, season, hour and first size explain only part of which fires escape. Wind, fuel moisture, slope and how fast the first engine arrives explain the rest, and none of them are in this record. Live data is where a copilot earns its keep, and the base rate is the floor it should have to argue up from.

    Method

    Records come from the NIFC WFIGS Incident Locations service, pulled November 18, 2026: every California incident typed as a wildfire, discovered 2022 to 2025. Of those, the ones with a recorded size (final acres, else incident size) form the cohort. Escaped means final size above 10 acres. Intervals are 95% Wilson intervals. Holdout: precedent rates built from 2022 to 2024, scored on 2025.

    Limits

    This is the interagency record for all agencies, not CAL FIRE’s own count, so it will not match their tally. Many local-government responses never get a size and drop out. Six contract counties run their own fire departments and are thinly covered. “Size at first report” is often a placeholder. Associations, not causes. Not for operational use.
